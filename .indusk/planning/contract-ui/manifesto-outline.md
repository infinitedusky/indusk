# Outline: Nothing here is self-reported

The outline beside [manifesto.md](manifesto.md): per section, what it does and
why it sits where it does, and where each claim comes from. Written at the
first revision, 2026-10-05; the first draft had none.

**Piece.** The argument for the InDusk interface, in one page. Occasion: the
direction "build the successor to Linear, with the contract as the key
primitive".

**Reader.** Not yet named by the author. Assumed here: a developer or an
engineering lead who has lived in a ticket tracker and is about to see the
demo ([indusk-demo](../indusk-demo/master.md)). To confirm.

**Arc.** The reader starts believing a good tracker is the answer. They end
holding that a status nobody can check is the problem, that a contract with
the running system replaces it, and that their own part shrinks to writing
promises and judging premises.

## Sections

1. **What Linear got right.** Grants the opponent its win, so the next section
   reads as a limit and not an attack. From the 2026-10-05 direction.
2. **Where it stops.** The one flaw: every status is a report. The agent
   paragraph ends on the aim's first sentence (agents whose work cannot be
   taken on trust), which is what makes the flaw urgent now.
3. **The primitive is the contract.** Three levels, each with its own truth,
   then the hierarchy, then why it self-corrects and toward what.
   - Premises: judged by a person on evidence. Names the evidence as the two
     inputs from the conversation of 2026-10-02/03: expectations of use,
     written in advance, and feedback in people's own words.
   - Promises: stated by a person in plain language (the aim's second
     sentence; the plain-English, behaviour-driven line of 2026-10-03), named
     before the code exists so the report arrives meaning something (why the
     loop is the wedge).
   - Phases: complete when tests pass. The plan behind one can live anywhere
     ("plans can be an extension; plans could come from Linear", 2026-10-02).
   - Last paragraph: the fixed point. A self-correcting system corrects toward
     what it is held to, so the promises are the person's.
4. **What a person does here.** The aim's seventh sentence, then its three
   consequences in the order a person meets them: what they are asked, why
   the screen is small (essential, not minimal; the phone), how they go
   deeper (one step more concrete; code last). Sits after the contract
   because each job is defined by a level of it.
5. **What follows.** Five consequences for the interface. One to three carry
   the argument; four and five are standards the build is held to.
   - 2 carries the telemetry point: the agent reads it, the person reads the
     incident.
   - 3 carries the aim's sixth sentence: plans, gates, tests before code and
     review exist for the promises.
6. **The test.** The verdict, as what a person does and says. The closing
   line is the aim's own measure of success.
7. **What this is not.** Three misreadings, each one a way to dismiss it: a
   dashboard, a test language, the end of planning. The dashboard line was
   first "there are no graphs to watch", which the author judged too strong
   (2026-10-05): graphs let a person understand and check the system. It now
   states the real difference in the author's own terms: in a telemetry tool
   telemetry is the output, and here it is an input, the nervous system that
   carries signals back to the agents that build the software. A person sees
   only what they could not close.

## The two triads

The conversation of 2026-10-02/03 named three things a person supplies:
promises, expectations, feedback. The manifesto's three levels are premises,
promises, phases. They are different cuts and both stand. They join at
premises: expectations and feedback are the evidence a premise is scored on.
If the author wants expectations and feedback as levels in their own right,
section 3 is where that changes.

## Left out on purpose

From the same conversations, and not interface claims: the wedge and the
expansion path, the small team against large telemetry vendors, the raise, the
product for people who do not read code beyond the one clause that a promise
is plain language, and the name BDD.
