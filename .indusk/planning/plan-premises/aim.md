# What InDusk is

The statement the premises plan calls "the aim": what this is for, in the
person's words, revised in place and dated. It was first written at the end of
a long conversation on the night of 2026-10-01, after the intake of eighteen
coworkers' AI processes and a day of explaining InDusk to people who wanted to
work on it. It is a draft and will keep changing; the revision log at the bottom
is the record.

## The statement

> InDusk is a development system for building software with agents that cannot
> be trusted. A person states what the software must do, as promises. Agents
> build it. The running software reports, through telemetry, whether each
> promise holds. When one breaks, the system says so and reopens the work that
> owns it. Everything else in InDusk, plans, gates, tests before code, review,
> exists to make the promises hold and to catch it when they do not. The
> interface has two jobs: write the promises, and manage the agents that keep
> them.

## How to read it

- Sentences four and five are the loop. The product conversation of 2026-10-01
  is the claim that those two can be sold on their own, with the rest arriving
  after.
- Sentence two carries the fixed point. A self-correcting system corrects
  toward whatever it was told to hold, so the promise is the person's even when
  an agent drafts the words. If that sentence weakens, the rest describes a
  system that corrects toward what the builder wanted.
- Sentence six is the demotion. Plans, gates, tests-first and review are in
  service of the promises, not the other way round. A plan source can be an
  extension; promises cannot.
- "Cannot be trusted" was judged a little strong on first reading and left as
  written. The alternative on the table was something like "whose work cannot
  be taken on trust". Open.

## What success looks like

Someone says: "this isn't doing that much, but it's so powerful." The aim is a
shift in mental model, not a feature count. The artifact that ships should be
the size of the argument.

## Candidate premises (drafted by the working session, not accepted)

What each sentence rests on, as things that could stop being true. The premises
plan says these are the person's; this list is a starting point for that, not
the list.

1. Telemetry can judge a promise: a behaviour worth promising can be marked, and
   a mark's absence or shape can say whether it held. Threatens sentences four
   and five.
2. Agents respect a refused write where they ignore prose. Threatens sentence
   six; if it fails, the gates are ceremony.
3. A person will write promises, given a place to write them, rather than
   tickets alone. Threatens sentences two and seven.
4. The meaning attached at planning time is worth more than what a telemetry
   vendor can infer from the exhaust afterwards. Threatens the loop being a
   wedge at all.
5. The system can be made small enough that the argument and the artifact are
   the same size. Threatens "what success looks like".
