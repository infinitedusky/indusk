# When a plan decides something is exempt, preserved, or excluded, encode that decision in the enforcement mechanism in the same commit — even if the mechanism can't reach it yet

A decision written up in prose ("we're treating X as preserved history, not residue") is not a decision the system holds — it's a decision a human remembers, until they don't. If an enforcement mechanism later widens its scope to reach the thing the prose exempted, the exemption has to already be encoded somewhere the mechanism reads, or the widening will flag something the project deliberately decided to keep.

**What happened:** `jj-residue-rip-out`'s Build Phase 2 explicitly ruled the changelog's historical jj references "preserved history," not residue to remove, and wrote that decision up in the impl. But the enforcement test's exemption list (`PRESERVED_HISTORY` in `scm-rip-out-grep.test.ts`) didn't gain a changelog entry — because at that point the test didn't scan `.md` files at all, so the omission had no way to surface. The gap was invisible until a later phase widened the scope to prose files specifically to catch jj residue in documentation ([[enforcement-grep-test-scope-has-two-independent-blind-spots]]) — at which point the changelog would have been flagged as a violation, because the decision to preserve it existed only in a phase's prose, not in the list the audit actually reads.

**Why it matters:** a mechanism's blind spots are, by definition, invisible while they're blind. The moment you widen a mechanism's reach, everything you decided was fine — but never told the mechanism about — becomes exposed at once, and it's ambiguous at that point whether each exposure is a real violation or a decision that never got encoded. Encoding the decision when you make it, not when the mechanism can act on it, removes that ambiguity in advance.

**The rule:** when a plan or ritual decides something is exempt, preserved, grandfathered, or intentionally excluded from an enforcement mechanism, add it to the mechanism's exemption/allow-list data structure in the same commit that makes the decision — even if the mechanism's current scope can't yet reach the thing being exempted. The exemption sits dormant and correct until scope widens to it, instead of being a fact that only existed in a commit message someone has to remember to go find.

See `.indusk/planning/jj-residue-rip-out/` for the full incident.
