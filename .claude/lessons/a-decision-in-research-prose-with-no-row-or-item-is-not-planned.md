# A decision recorded only as prose in research.md, with no trajectory row and no checklist item, silently never lands

model-per-phase's research.md (`.indusk/planning/archive/model-per-phase/research.md:33`) decided "the planner decides each phase's model when it writes the phase" — but that plan's impl only landed the tier *config* and its validator rule, never a planner-skill step or template line to actually write a `**Tier**:` line under each phase. The gap sat invisible for one full plan cycle and was only caught when a later plan (`plan-review-subagent`) wrote its own impl with no tier lines, and someone noticed.

Why: research.md and ADRs hold prose decisions, but only trajectory rows and checklist items are structurally enforced (validator hooks, gate checks, `pnpm test`). A decision stated in prose with no corresponding row/item has no mechanism forcing it into the impl — it's trusted to make the jump from "decided" to "built" by memory alone, and that jump is exactly the kind of thing falsification and cleanup are supposed to catch within a plan, but neither ritual re-reads the *originating research* for decisions that never got a row.

How to apply: when closing out a plan (falsify/cleanup) or when authoring a new impl, grep the plan's own research.md and ADR for decision bullets, and check each one traces to at least one Test Trajectory row or checklist item. A decision with neither is not planned — it only looks planned. This is also a good candidate check for the audit step this plan (`plan-review-subagent`) is building: "which decision in the research or ADR has no row that proves it?" is close to the auditor's fixed question list already, and this incident is a concrete instance of exactly the failure mode the audit step exists to catch across plans, not just within one.
