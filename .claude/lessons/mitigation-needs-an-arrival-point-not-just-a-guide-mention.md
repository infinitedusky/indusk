# A deferred-verification mitigation that's only recorded in a guide page is not a mitigation — wire it into the process step that has to act on it at the right moment

When an impl's Deferred Verification section defers a claim with a `mitigation:` that amounts to "record X in a guide/doc so future readers know," that satisfies the letter of having a mitigation but not its purpose — nobody reads a guide page at the specific moment the recorded obligation needs to be acted on. A promise with no arrival point in an actual process step will never fire, no matter how clearly it's documented.

Seen in dusk (lifecycle-rebalance, commit 09d4f4ec): U1's mitigation for "Shape flags what a thoughtful reviewer would flag" was originally just "recorded in the Shape guide so the obligation is visible to whoever runs the next plan." That's a real gap — the guide is not something anyone opens during `/retrospective`, so the finding/false-positive counts the mitigation depends on would never actually get collected, and the "two consecutive plans of human-judged-wrong findings" trigger could never fire. The fix wasn't more guide prose; it was wiring the obligation directly into `/retrospective`'s Quality Audit step (asking for both counts by name, and for the two-in-a-row streak explicitly) and into `planner.md`'s retrospective template (a slot for the numbers). The guide page still explains *why*, but the actual collection now happens where retrospectives are actually written.

When authoring or reviewing a mitigation for a deferred/unverifiable claim, ask: *does this mitigation name the specific skill/step/template that will act on it, or does it just say "record this somewhere"?* If a human has to remember to go read a guide at the right moment for the mitigation to work, it isn't wired — find the process step that already runs at that moment (a retrospective, a phase gate, a checklist template) and put the obligation there instead of, or in addition to, the explanatory doc.
