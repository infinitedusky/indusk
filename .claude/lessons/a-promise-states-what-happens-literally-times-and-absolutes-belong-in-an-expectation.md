# A promise states what happens, literally — a time or an absolute ("under a minute", "never waits") is the purpose and belongs in a brief's Expectation, met by degree

Sandy, 2026-10-17, rewriting the tests-never-make-us-wait promises: "a plan runs the tests it writes, what its changes reach and what must not break" can be checked and does not drift; "the suite finishes within two minutes" or "never waits" cannot be kept as a promise, it is what the promise is for. Put the time or outcome in the brief's Expectations (measure + look), which are met by degree and never break.

Why: the old everyday-suite-stays-fast promise read broken at 150 s while nothing had been done wrong, and it was replaced. How to apply: when drafting a promise, ask whether its sentence names an action the system takes or an outcome with a number; move numbers and "never/always wait" wording to an Expectation.
