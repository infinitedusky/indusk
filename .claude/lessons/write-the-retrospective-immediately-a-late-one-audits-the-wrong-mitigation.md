# A retrospective written long after impl-complete doesn't just risk being late — the deferred-verification mitigation it audits may have already been silently replaced by a different mechanism nobody wrote down

indusk-makeover's retrospective was written 53 days after the impl completed. Its Deferred Verification row U1 ("scheduled 2-week review" of the compressed CLAUDE.md's effectiveness) had two problems layered on top of each other: the review was never actually scheduled anywhere (no owner, no calendar trigger — the `mitigation-needs-an-arrival-point-not-just-a-guide-mention` failure mode), AND by the time anyone looked, a completely different mechanism — the per-close compaction step, built for unrelated reasons in a later plan — had ended up being the thing that actually kept CLAUDE.md's size in check. The retrospective had to be rewritten to name the mechanism that actually held, not the one that was originally planned to hold.

**Why the delay compounds the mitigation-arrival-point problem rather than just adding to it:** a retrospective written promptly would have found "the scheduled review never happened" as a clean, single defect — fix the process, move on. A retrospective written 53 days later instead has to reverse-engineer which of several things that happened in the meantime is actually responsible for the outcome looking fine, because "looking fine" and "the original mitigation worked" had already come apart without anyone noticing at the moment they diverged. The longer the gap, the more candidate explanations accumulate, and the harder it becomes to tell "the mitigation worked" from "something unrelated happened to also fix it" from "nothing broke yet."

**How to apply:** write the retrospective in the same session the impl completes, or the very next one — not as a policy for its own sake, but because a prompt retrospective audits the mitigation that was actually in effect at the time, while a delayed one has to audit whatever accumulated state exists by the time someone gets to it, which may silently no longer be the thing the impl's own documents claim. When a retrospective genuinely must be delayed, treat "does the originally-recorded mitigation still describe what's actually holding" as its own explicit check, separate from "did the mitigation ever fire" — the indusk-makeover case shows these can diverge independently.

See `.indusk/planning/archive/indusk-makeover/retrospective.md` (U1, U2) for the concrete case.
